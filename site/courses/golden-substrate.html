§1  The seed, the gap, the keystone

Everything rests on one quadratic, x² − x − 1, with roots the golden pair φ = (1+√5)/2 and ψ = (1−√5)/2 = −φ−1. Write τ = φ−1 = φ − 1 for the small golden unit. Two derived constants run through the whole course:

SC-A1–A7 φψ = −1,  φ+ψ = 1,  √5 = φ + φ−1,  τ² + τ = 1
gap = φ−4 = (7 − 3√5)/2,   K² = 1 − gap = (3√5 − 5)/2 The gap is the course's recurring small number; K is the real root of the K-seed x⁴ + 5x² − 5, and Kφ = 51/4, so K generates ℚ(51/4). All of this is decided at the squared level — no fourth root ever crosses a decision boundary.

In plain terms, the gap is the course's unit of shrinkage: φ−4 ≈ 0.146, its recurring small number. The complement K² = 1 − gap ≈ 0.854 is what survives a gap-sized contraction, and K = √(1 − gap) ≈ 0.924 is no ornament: it returns as the height of rung 2 (Lesson 2) and as the radius of the helix (Lesson 3). The whole course is machined from φ and this one small number.

The companion matrix of the seed is the keystone R = 0111, and the corpus derives rather than posits it: φ is the smallest Perron root above 1 of a 2×2 primitive non-negative integer matrix forced. Everything downstream needs only the one relation R² = R + I.

§2  The gate iteration, declared

The source note used the gate through its fixed-point polynomial without ever printing the map. This is the one place the substrate makes a genuine choice, so it is stated as a declared object — and it is pinned, not free: it is the unique reading under which all the corpus's gate identities hold at once.

Declaration · SC-F2 fC(x) = C/(1+x),   fixed points solve gC(x) = x² + x − C = 0
u± = ½(−1 ± √D),  D = 1 + 4C,   multiplier m = fC′(u) = −C/(1+u)² The flip is the sign change of D at C = −¼: real fixed points (terrain face) become complex (rotation face). Height will parametrize the first; angle the second.

Why iterate at all? Because a gate is meant to be run — feed a number in, read one out, repeat, and see where it settles. Take the golden gate C = 1, so f1(x) = 1/(1+x), and turn the crank from x0 = 1: you get ½, then 2/3, then 3/5, 5/8, 8/13, … — consecutive Fibonacci ratios, the continued-fraction convergents of the golden ratio. They close in on the attracting fixed point u1 = τ = 0.618…, the root of g1(x) = x² + x − 1. The smallest gate, run by hand, literally writes down τ forced.

How fast it settles is fixed by the multiplier m1 = −C/(1+u1)² = −1/(1+τ)² = −φ−2 ≈ −0.382. Its magnitude is below 1, so the gate contracts; its sign is negative, so the iterates straddle τ, alternating below and above (0.5, 0.667, 0.6, 0.625 around 0.618). That fold across the fixed point is the seed of the whole lesson: a real gate can fold (m < 0) or hold (|m| = 1), but it cannot spiral.

§3  The two multipliers are reciprocals

Here is the structural fact the rest of the lesson turns on. For every C ≠ 0 the two fixed-point multipliers satisfy a product law and a real trace law:

Prop · SC-F2–F3 m+ m− = 1,    m + 1/m = −1/C − 2 ∈ ℝ By Vieta on gC: u++u− = −1 and u+u− = −C, so (1+u+)(1+u−) = −C, and the multipliers multiply to C²/C² = 1. The partner of any multiplier m is exactly 1/m.

Both identities are forced and machine-verified for generic symbolic C. Read them together: the two multipliers are reciprocal and their sum is a real number fixed by C alone. Reciprocity has teeth: wherever one fixed point attracts (|m| < 1), its partner repels by the exact reciprocal amount (|1/m| > 1) — no gate owns two attractors. That perfect balance is what §4 turns into an impossibility.

§4  The obstruction: no single real gate is a helix

A helix would need a fixed point that both rotates and contracts — a multiplier with 0 < |m| < 1 and m not real. The reciprocity law forbids it outright.

Thm · SC-F2–F4 · HX27–28 write m = s·eiϑ, s > 0.  Then Im(m + 1/m) = (s − 1/s)·sinϑ
m + 1/m real  ⇒  s = 1  or  sinϑ = 0 Either |m| = 1 (pure rotation, on the flip's rotation face) or m is real (pure contraction, on the terrain face) — never both. No real gate level C linearizes to a genuine spiral. forced

Picture the multiplier as an arrow of length s at angle ϑ: contraction shortens it (s < 1), rotation tilts it (ϑ ≠ 0). The law pins m + 1/m to the real axis, and an arrow together with its reciprocal stays real only if the arrow is exactly unit length (all tilt, no shrink) or exactly horizontal (all shrink, no tilt). A spiral wants a short tilted arrow — the one combination the substrate forbids.

This is the exact content behind the partner paper's slogan that a helix cannot couple to a copy of itself. The flip identity is the obstruction.

§5  The minimal way around: the K-seed straddler

If one gate cannot do both jobs, the two faces of the flip must be supplied by a single object that straddles it. In the corpus catalog exactly one seed does: the K-seed x⁴ + 5x² − 5, whose roots split into a real pair (terrain) and an imaginary pair (the quarter-turn).

Cor · SC-H7–H8 roots of x⁴ + 5x² − 5:  ±K (terrain, arg 0/π),  ±iβ (rotation, arg ±π/2)
the two faces multiply to the field prime:  K²β² = 5,   β² = φ²√5 = M(K-seed) Real magnitude K, imaginary magnitude β: terrain growth and a genuine quarter-turn in one seed. Its charge set completes ℤ/4ℤ — the arguments {0, π/2, π, 3π/2}. Uniqueness of the straddler is a corpus result (paper 13); the root data is forced here.

So the substrate's helicity has a precise price: it is carried not by a gate but by the K-seed's two faces, which reappear in Lesson 3 as the radius K and in Lesson 4 as the modulus and phase of one complex number.

Q1Prove m+m− = 1 from Vieta alone — no calculus beyond the multiplier formula.
answer

The multiplier is m = −C/(1+u)², so m+m− = C² / [(1+u+)(1+u−)]². Expand the denominator with Vieta (u++u− = −1, u+u− = −C): (1+u+)(1+u−) = 1 + (−1) + (−C) = −C. So the product is C²/(−C)² = 1.

Q2In one line, why can no single real gate C both rotate and contract?
answer

Because m + 1/m = −1/C − 2 is real, and with m = s·eiϑ its imaginary part (s − 1/s)·sinϑ vanishes only if s = 1 (unimodular — pure rotation) or sinϑ = 0 (real — pure contraction). A spiral needs both s < 1 and sinϑ ≠ 0, which is exactly what is excluded.
