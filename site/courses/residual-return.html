Number Fields as Learning Geometry

One number field, read three ways and glued by a single exact rational metric — then made to learn, to speak, and to price what it learns. Residual return is the mechanism common to all three.

This booklet threads the Residual Return series — three papers — into one path. The protocol is half the subject, and it is the corpus's throughout:

1. read the paper's source  →  2. run the exact-arithmetic check  →  3. teach only what passed

The thesis is a single sentence the papers state outright: residual → 0 is the universal learned signal. A field learns by driving its trace-form residual r = x − Px to zero; a matrix algebra speaks by driving the return operator ℒ(X) = RX + XR − X to zero; and one scalar λ prices how much explained structure a growth step must earn against what it costs. Every load-bearing claim below carries a tag naming how strongly it is held — forced proved or exact-verified, computed exhaustive over a stated range, declared a principled invariance-free choice, open unresolved, established imported from the literature. Lesson 01 · The Vector Substrate

One Field, Three Faces

A number field ℚ(θ) is a vector space, a matrix algebra, and a Euclidean lattice at once — and a single exact rational matrix G is the metric of all three.

33/33exact checks passed

Here is the question the whole course answers: what would it mean for a piece of mathematics to learn? The framework's answer is disarmingly concrete. Take a number field — the numbers you reach by adjoining one algebraic number θ to the rationals — and equip it with a single exact ruler. Then "learning" is nothing more mysterious than driving a measured error to zero, and "understanding" a new number is the exact event that its error vanishes. This first lesson builds the ruler. Everything after it is that ruler used in earnest.

§1  Three coordinate systems, one object

Let ℚ(θ) be a number field of degree n = [ℚ(θ):ℚ], with θ a root of an irreducible monic mθ ∈ ℤ[x]. It admits three classical descriptions that are not three analogies but one object in three coordinate systems:

  • a ℚ-vector space of dimension n, basis {1, θ, …, θn−1} — a number is a list of n rationals;
  • a commutative matrix algebra in Mn(ℚ) — the regular representation ρ, under which multiplication by θ is the companion matrix C(mθ);
  • a full-rank Euclidean lattice in ℝn via the Minkowski embedding σ = (σ₁, …, σn) — the n ways to send θ to one of its conjugates.

A single rational bilinear form glues the three faces, and it is the object the rest of the course computes with. It is the trace form: pair two numbers by multiplying them and taking the field trace — the sum over all n embeddings.

eq:traceform T(x, y) = TrK/ℚ(xy) = ∑ᵢ σᵢ(x)·σᵢ(y) The trace form: one rational bilinear form. The trace collapses all n conjugates to a single rational, so membership in the field becomes decidable by exact rational arithmetic — no floating point, no roots. That decidability is the entire point.

§2  The gluing matrix G = MᵀM

Pick a basis {ωᵢ}. Recording the trace form on it gives one matrix Gᵢⱼ = Tr(ωᵢωⱼ), and this single matrix is the bridge: it is at once the Gram matrix of the algebra's pairing and the Gram matrix of the embedded lattice, because each embedding σᵢ is a ring homomorphism and the trace is their sum.

Thm 2.13 G = MᵀM,   det G = dK  (integral basis),   signature (r₁+r₂, r₂) — positive definite iff totally real M is the embedding matrix Mki = σk(ωᵢ). Reading G = MᵀM off the definition takes one line: Gᵢⱼ = ∑k σk(ωᵢ)σk(ωⱼ). The determinant of the trace form is the field discriminant on an integral basis. When complex places enter (r₂ > 0) the positive-definite companion is the Hermitian form G₂ = M*M, det = |dK|.

Make it concrete in the golden field. In ℚ(√5) with basis {1, φ} (φ = (1+√5)/2, the root of x² − x − 1), every entry of G is a trace you can do in your head:

test_trace_form G = 2113,   det G = 5 = dℚ(√5),   sign(det G) = (−1)r₂ Read straight off three traces: Tr(1) = 2 (each of two embeddings fixes 1), Tr(φ) = 1 (since φ + ψ = 1, the sum of the two roots), and Tr(φ²) = Tr(φ + 1) = 3. ℤ[φ] is the maximal order, so det G = 2·3 − 1 = 5 is exactly the discriminant, and the sign of the determinant is the definiteness flag — a fact Lesson 4 will read as the sign of a discriminant.

§3  The spectral bridge

Why should a metric matrix know anything about heights and dynamics? Because the matrix face and the lattice face are one operator in two bases. The Minkowski embedding diagonalises the regular representation: Mρ(x)M⁻¹ = diag(σ₁(x), …, σn(x)) for every x at once. So the eigenvalues of the companion matrix ρ(θ) are exactly the Galois conjugates of θ — its roots.

One consequence is the hinge of the whole series. The Mahler measure — the product of root moduli that escape the unit circle — is therefore a spectral invariant of the companion matrix: M(θ) = ∏|λ|>1 |λ| over the eigenvalues λ of ρ(θ) forced. The same rational matrix that carries the metric carries the height. This single identification is why the geometry of Lessons 1–3 and the cost of Lesson 4 are one object. Worked once: in ℚ(√5), M diagonalising ρ(φ) gives eigenvalues φ and ψ, so charpoly(ρ(φ)) = x² − x − 1 = mφ and M(φ) = φ computed.

§4  The discriminant is an information volume

Before the metric learns anything, it already prices the field itself. Read G as a Fisher information matrix: put an isotropic Gaussian on the Minkowski space with mean Ma, and the Fisher information in the coordinate a is exactly MᵀM = G forced. This is a classical location-family fact, assembled here rather than proved new — and it makes the trace form a natural Riemannian metric of a statistical manifold, the reading Lesson 4 turns into an exchange rate.

prop:discvol volJeffreys = √(det G) = √|dK| = covol(σ(𝒪K)) The Jeffreys prior density of a Fisher metric is √(det Fisher); for the constant metric G it integrates over one lattice cell to √|dK|, the Minkowski covolume. So the square root of the discriminant is the natural information volume of one integer cell: a more ramified field is, in a precise statistical sense, larger to learn.

The numbers are legible. ℚ(√5) has cell volume √5 ≈ 2.236; ℚ(√2) has 2√2 ≈ 2.828; the more ramified ℚ(∛2) has 6√3 ≈ 10.39 — about 4.6 times a golden cell. "More ramified" is here literally "more information volume per lattice cell" computed.

§5  Capture is a vanishing residual

Now the metric learns. Fix a forced basis: a full-rank B ∈ ℚn×m whose columns are coordinate vectors of algebraic integers, spanning col(B) — the model's current dictionary. Provided BᵀGB is invertible, define the trace-form projector and residual:

eq:projector · eq:capture P = B(BᵀGB)⁻¹BᵀG,    r(x) = x − Px
x ∈ col(B)  ⇔  r(x) = 0  ⇔  ‖r(x)‖G² = rᵀGr = 0 P² = P and BᵀG·r = 0 (the residual is G-orthogonal to the dictionary): pure novelty, carrying no component already explained. The capture criterion is forced — the last equivalence needs G positive definite, i.e. a totally real field. "Understanding" is, in this framework, exactly a zero residual: the predicate is forced, but reading it as "learned" is a declared naming choice, and it is an equation either way, never a threshold.

Watch it decide, entirely in ℚ. Take the forced basis B = (0, 1)ᵀ — the line ℚφ — so BᵀGB = 3 and P = ⅓0013. The element 2φ = (0, 2)ᵀ has P(2φ) = 2φ, residual 0: captured. The element √5 = 2φ − 1 = (−1, 2)ᵀ projects to (0, ⅓)ᵀ, so r = (−1, ⅓)ᵀ ≠ 0 with ‖r‖G² = 5/3 > 0: off-axis novelty. Every quantity is an exact rational — the residual norm is read only as a real magnitude ("how far off"), never thresholded to make the decision.

answer

Q2A second worked build. In ℚ(√2), basis {1, √2}, compute G from the traces, read det G, and give the field's Jeffreys information volume.
answer

Tr(1) = 2; Tr(√2) = √2 + (−√2) = 0; Tr((√2)²) = Tr(2) = 4. So G = 2004, diagonal because 1 and √2 are trace-orthogonal, and det G = 8 = dℚ(√2). The information volume is √(det G) = √8 = 2√2 ≈ 2.828 — a touch larger than the golden cell's √5, because ℚ(√2) is the more ramified field computed.

Q3Why is the equivalence ‖r‖G² = 0 ⇔ r = 0 guaranteed only on a totally real field, and what replaces G when it fails?
answer

The forward direction needs G positive definite, which holds iff r₂ = 0 (totally real). With a complex place the signature is (r₁+r₂, r₂) — indefinite — and a nonzero r can satisfy rᵀGr = 0, so the norm-zero test would report a false capture. The fix the paper prescribes is the Hermitian companion G₂ = M*M, positive definite with det = |dK|; the whole projector/residual calculus runs verbatim over it. (Lesson 4 shows this is the same event as the sign flip of a discriminant.)

Q4The residual is G-orthogonal to col(B). In one sentence, why does that make it exactly the object to be learned?
answer

Because BᵀG·r = 0 means r pairs to zero, under the trace, against the entire current dictionary — it is the part of x invisible to the model in the trace pairing (its dual-basis direction), so capturing it is exactly adjoining the missing direction and nothing already known.

Lesson 02 · Residual Return

Residual as Loss, Height as Capacity

The residual is the error signal; learning drives it to zero by growing the basis — and arithmetic height keeps that growth provably finite, with a nonzero cost floor.

carries forward
56/56exact checks passed

Lesson 1 built a ruler that says captured or not captured. That is a static verdict; a learner has to do something when the verdict is "not captured" — it must grow until the novelty is explained. Two dangers appear the moment growth is allowed. Growth could run forever, defeating the point of an exact finite model; and growth could be triggered by noise. This lesson turns the static criterion into a streaming loop, then fences it with a single arithmetic idea — the height of a number — that makes both dangers provably bounded.

§1  The residual is the loss

In place of a differentiable loss minimised by gradient descent, the framework uses the exact residual as its error signal. There is no learning rate and no approximation; the Pythagorean decomposition splits each observation cleanly into what the model already explains and what it does not:

eq:pythagoras ‖x‖G² = ‖Px‖G² + ‖r(x)‖G² Immediate from G-orthogonality of Px and r. Learning drives the second term to zero by enlarging col(B). The streaming learner makes this a loop: residual is loss, capture is r → 0, and growth is a gated propose-for-confirm protocol whose sole basis mutator (confirm) appends a SHA-256 witness chain. Every decision is over ℚ; a float raises TypeError before it touches the decision core forced.

Three operations keep the mutation honest. observe computes the residual and never touches the basis; propose is pure — it returns a candidate seed only after the residual direction has persisted and the capacity gate admits it; confirm is the one line that adjoins a column, re-derives P, and appends a tamper-evident hash link. A trained model is therefore a short replayable record, not an opaque blob: a restart is exact re-derivation from the seeds, agreeing as an operator and not merely to a tolerance.

§2  Growth: basis enlargement, and the compositum

The learner's ambient field never changes silently — a deliberate invariant. Sometimes the residual is still an element of the current field K, and growth is just a new column. But sometimes the observed quantity is forced into a strictly larger field: then a new field is mathematically a new learner. To name the seed exactly the framework reads its minimal polynomial off linear algebra — mα is the largest invariant factor of xI − ρ(α), from the Smith normal form, computed rationally and rootlessly forced. Two construction modes, one decision:

  • Disjoint: W = K ⊗ L with Kronecker Gram GW = GK ⊗ GL — and confirm() ships a literal assert gram == kron(...), a proposition running as a runtime self-check forced.
  • Non-disjoint: the true compositum of smaller degree, built by factoring the candidate's minimal polynomial over K. This resolves the open problem the substrate paper flagged (its O4) in the bounded-degree case computed.

The non-disjoint witness is worth seeing, because it is exactly where the naive picture fails. Over K = ℚ(√2), adjoin β = √2 + √3, whose minimal polynomial x⁴ − 10x² + 1 factors over K as (x² − 2√2 x − 1)(x² + 2√2 x − 1). So β satisfies a quadratic over K, not a quartic: the true compositum ℚ(√2, √3) has degree 4, not the tensor degree 2·4 = 8. A Kronecker 8×8 Gram would describe a space that does not exist. The construction picks the primitive element θ = √2 + β, whose degree-6 operator polynomial factors as (x² − 3)(x⁴ − 22x² + 25), and selects the genuine factor mθ = x⁴ − 22x² + 25, rejecting the spurious x² − 3 computed.

§3  The height gate: Northcott, decided in ℤ

Unbounded growth would defeat an exact finite model. Capacity is bounded by arithmetic height, which makes the admissible seed set not merely finite but listable.

Thm 5.2 · Prop 5.5 M(θ) = height(θ)deg θ,   M(p)² ≤ ∑ᵢ cᵢ² ∈ ℤ  (Landau)
admissible ⇔ deg(m) ≤ Dmax  and  ch(m) ≤ Hmax Landau's inequality bounds the (float) Mahler measure by an exact integer — the sum of squared coefficients; Northcott's theorem makes finitely many algebraic integers of bounded degree and height established. So the gate is decided entirely in ℤ from (degree, coefficient-height) — the Mahler value never enters forced. The gate capacity_decision returns GROW / STOP / REJECT.

Finiteness is concrete, not asymptotic. Under the tiny budget (Dmax, Hmax) = (2, 1) the admissible monic integer polynomials number exactly 3 + 9 = 12: three linear and nine quadratic. Two of the quadratics are irreducible new fields (x² − x − 1 = φ and x² + x − 1); x² + 1 cuts out ℚ(i); the rest are reducible and define no new field. Raising Hmax to 24 first admits 2√6 (x² − 24). The budget is precisely the dial that bounds an explicitly enumerable dictionary.

The float never crosses the gate. The exact value M(√7) = 7 is returned by a root finder as 7.000000000000001 — strictly above 7. A naive test "M ≤ 7" would reject √7 on rounding noise; the integer gate, reading only (deg, ch), cannot. This is Lesson 1's discipline cashed in at a decision boundary.

§4  The cost floor: Smyth, and the reciprocal core

Frame growth as information gained versus spent: gain = ‖r‖G², cost = λ·log M(θ), where log M is the seed's topological entropy (its intrinsic description length). One grows precisely when the code saved exceeds the code spent — a minimum-description-length test. Then how cheap can a genuine seed be?

thm:smythfloor non-reciprocal, non-cyclotomic  ⇒  M(p) ≥ μₛ = 1.3247179… Smyth's theorem: μₛ is the plastic number, the real root of x³ − x − 1, and the smallest Mahler measure of a non-reciprocal algebraic integer established. Hence the cost of any non-reciprocal genuine seed is at least λ·log μₛ > 0 forced. The worked seeds φ (x² − x − 1), 2√6 (x² − 24), √7 (x² − 7) are all non-reciprocal, so their floors are unconditional.

Why insist on a nonzero floor? Because a floor of 0 grows on noise. In ℚ(√5) take the residual centroid r = (1/10)√5 — an exact rational multiple of the off-axis direction, trace-zero and genuinely off-axis, yet tiny: ‖r‖G² = (1/100)·10 = 1/10. The shipped gate (floor 0) would GROW on it — noise that merely happens to land on the lattice — while the Smyth floor λ·log μₛ = 0.562 STOPs it, since 1/10 < 0.562 computed. That is the concrete job of the floor.

The reciprocal case is the unsolved core. Every known Mahler measure in (1, μₛ) comes from a reciprocal integer, and Salem numbers and units arise naturally as seeds; for them there is no proven uniform positive floor — only Dobrowolski's degree-dependent bound, which tends to 1, or Lehmer's conjectural μL = 1.17628… open. Lesson 4 closes exactly this gap on the framework's own emission image, without touching general Lehmer.

All the pieces run in one loop. Ambient field K = ℚ(√2+√3), forced basis {1, θ}; the off-axis quantity 2√6 = θ² − 5 arrives repeatedly. The detector scores each observation by the exact residual norm and alerts only once a calibration gate opens — detection is decoupled from acting:

Q3Why does raising a basis column by a nonzero rational scale leave P — hence every residual and every capture verdict — unchanged?

The reciprocal seeds (Salem numbers, units) leave the general positive-floor claim open; that is Lehmer's problem, and Lesson 4 resolves it only on the emission image.

Lesson 03 · Residual Return

ℒ(X) = RX + XR − X

The same residual → 0 mechanism becomes a language over the Clifford algebra Cl(2,0) ≅ M₂(ℝ), where the kernel of one operator is the lexicon.

carries forward
21/21exact checks passed

So far "residual → 0" has meant a number field learning. The surprising claim of this lesson is that the identical mechanism, moved to a small matrix algebra, becomes a language: a growing dictionary of meanings in which two tokens mean the same thing exactly when they return to the same residue. Nothing here is analogy-by-hand — the two faces share a literal arithmetic object and a byte-identical witness chain. This lesson builds the second face, then shows the bridge.

§1  The exact carrier

The language layer lives in a separate, strictly one-way module — it reads the number-field engine only through a bridge and is never imported back. Its carrier is Cl(2,0) ≅ M₂(ℝ), the algebra of 2×2 real matrices in disguise, realised exactly over ℚ (no float in any decision). A holding is a four-number code X = a + b e₁ + c e₂ + d i with a, b, c, d ∈ ℚ, where e₁² = e₂² = 1, i = e₁e₂, i² = −1, and mat(X) = a+cb−db+da−c.

Every question about a holding is an exact equality over ℚ. The gate P₀ = ½(1 + e₂) is a symmetric idempotent — its idempotence defect vanishes exactly, so it is "captured" by that reading — yet it is not conformal: its trace-free Gram residue is ½e₂ ≠ 0. By contrast the unit 1 is conformal, and the keystone R below has type {generator} because it satisfies the golden law X² = X + 1 exactly computed. Each of these is an "==" decision over ℚ, never a tolerance — the same zero-tolerance discipline as Lesson 1's capture.

§2  The φ-keystone return operator

The keystone is R = ½ + e₁ − ½e₂, i.e. mat(R) = 0111 — the companion matrix of x² − x − 1, the very matrix ρ(φ) that opened Lesson 1. It satisfies the golden law R² = R + I, and mat(R) is symmetric. The return operator is its Sylvester self-action — apply R on both sides and subtract the identity:

eq:Lop ℒ(X) = RX + XR − X ℒ measures how far X is from being reproduced by the two-sided action of the keystone. It is computed by the exact geometric product and cross-checked against an independent matrix-route oracle, ruling out a single-route bug. R itself is not in the kernel (ℒ(R) = 5/2 + e₁ − ½e₂ ≠ 0) forced.

Why subtract exactly the identity? The Sylvester map S(X) = RX + XR alone shifts every eigenvalue up by one; the −X re-centres "no change" at zero, so that captured means literally "the keystone's two-sided action returns X to itself." R is the generator of that action, which is why R itself is the one holding ℒ cannot fix: it is the source of the dynamics, not one of its settled meanings.

§3  The kernel is the lexicon

The payoff is an exact, hand-rolled rational nullspace — not a floating SVD. Where does the returned residue live?

thm:kernel ker ℒ = span{ e₁ + 2e₂,   i }   (exactly 2-dimensional)
ℒ = S − id,   spec(S) = {2φ, 1, 1, 2ψ}  ⇒  spec(ℒ) = {√5, 0, 0, −√5} The Sylvester eigenvalue argument confirms the dimension: S(X) = RX + XR has eigenvalues λa + λb over the eigenvalues {φ, ψ} of R (with φ + ψ = 1). Subtracting the identity sends the two middle eigenvalues 1 to 0, so ℒ has eigenvalue 0 with multiplicity 2 forced. Note the spectrum {√5, 0, 0, −√5} — it will reappear in Lesson 4 as the golden-gate trifurcation.

Calling that kernel the φ-slack, the lexicon is the source's own reading: the dimension is proved forced, the interpretation is named declared. The commit is the exact idempotent projector onto ker ℒ (a value-pinned matrix, not merely some P² = P), and a learned dictionary value is exactly a committed residue. Two tokens with the same exact residue merge to one entry; distinct residues stay distinct forced.

This is the very shape of Lesson 1's trace-form projector P: an exact idempotent onto a landing space, with "learned" meaning "lands in the space." The one honest difference between the faces is which piece grows. Here the landing space is fixed — the 2-dimensional φ-slack — and the model (the lexicon) grows inside it; on the number-field face the landing space col(B) is itself what grows. Same projector calculus, opposite thing moving.
