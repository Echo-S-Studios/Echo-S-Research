Into an Algebra

Take one matrix R with R² = R + I. Everything else is forced: a ring, an 𝔰𝔩₂, a Mahler-graded semiring — read by two characters, coupled at a golden floor, then made reference-free.

Module 1 gave the objects and the two walls that confine them. This module gives them an algebra. The seed is the golden ratio wearing a matrix costume: the companion matrix R of x² − x − 1. Accept it and the relation it satisfies, and you do not get to choose what follows — the ring it generates, the Lie algebra it acts on, the two numbers that grade its objects, and the boundary where that grading collapses are all consequences. The four papers below build that structure once, then read it three ways.

1. one matrix, one relation  →  the algebra 𝔄 in three layers  →  2. the operators are a λ-ring, two characters  →  3. charge couples to measure  →  4. delete the reference ray

Every load-bearing claim carries a tag naming how strongly it is held — forced a theorem or exact computation, computed exhaustive over a stated range, declared a principled naming or modelling choice, plausible evidence short of proof, open unresolved, established imported from the literature. The tags are the mathematics, not decoration: a suggestive picture that has not been proved earns declared, never forced.

Lesson 01 · the emission algebra 𝔄

One Matrix, One Relation → the Algebra 𝔄

A single companion matrix and the identity it satisfies grow three exact layers — a ring, an 𝔰𝔩₂, a semiring — welded by one trace duality. Almost nothing is posited; the structure unfolds from the seed.

d1–d7exact-arithmetic scripts, over ℤ, ℚ(√5), ℚ(51/4)

§1  The seed and the keystone

The golden ratio φ = (1+√5)/2 satisfies φ² = φ + 1. Carry that number inside linear algebra as the companion matrix of x² − x − 1, and the scalar identity becomes a matrix identity — the keystone.

keystone R = 0111,   R² = R + I,   Tr R = 1,  det R = −1,  spec R = {φ, ψ} The two eigenvalues are the roots of x² − x − 1: φ and its conjugate ψ = (1−√5)/2 = −1/φ. Any polynomial identity satisfied by φ is automatically satisfied by R (with I standing in for 1). The whole first layer is this dictionary made precise. forced

Iterating the one reduction R² = R + I forces Fibonacci to appear — not by coincidence, but as a theorem. Reducing every R² gives R³ = 2R + I, R⁴ = 3R + 2I, R⁵ = 5R + 3I, and in general:

power law · D1 Rn = FnR + Fn−1I = Fn−1FnFnFn+1  (all n ∈ ℤ) Whence the classical identities as trace and determinant: Tr(Rn) = Ln (Lucas), det(Rn) = (−1)n, Cassini Fn−1Fn+1 − Fn² = (−1)n, and Binet Fn = (φn − ψn)/√5. Verified symbolically for all n and spot-checked n ∈ [−16, 16]. forced

§2  Layer one — the golden order

The powers of R live in the ℤ-span of I and R. That span is a ring, and it is exactly the right one: ℤ[R] ≅ ℤ[φ] = 𝒪ℚ(√5), the ring of integers of the golden field. Its discriminant is disc(x² − x − 1) = 5, the fundamental discriminant of ℚ(√5) (because 5 ≡ 1 mod 4), so the index [𝒪 : ℤ[φ]] is 1 — the order is maximal, nothing missing. forced The golden seed is not arbitrary but optimal: a clumsier seed like 2φ (discriminant 20) lands in a defective sub-order of index 2.

§3  Layer two — an 𝔰𝔩₂ with golden root length

How does R act on its own surroundings — the whole 4-dimensional space M₂ of matrices — through the commutator adR(X) = [R, X]? Its spectrum is {0, 0, +√5, −√5}: a 2-dimensional centre fixed by R, and two root directions along which it shears at rate ±√5. The traceless part assembles into the most important small Lie algebra in mathematics.

D4 · signature H := 2R − I,  Tr H = 0,  H² = 5I,  [N+, N−] = √5·H The layer is 𝔰𝔩₂ of Lorentzian signature (2,1) — two boost directions, one rotation, two null root vectors — with root length the golden coupling √5 = φ − ψ = φ + φ−1. The trace form ⟨X,Y⟩ = Tr(XY) is diag(10, 10, −2). Diagonalising the Cartan needs exactly √5 and no more. forced

§4  The trace duality that welds the layers

Subtract from 2Rn its own trace and a small miracle occurs: the remainder collapses onto a single ray. The deviation operator Xn = 2Rn − LnI equals FnH — the entire Fibonacci ladder is one Cartan axis, sampled at Fibonacci scale. Its hyperbolic length is the duality:

D6 · duality ½·Tr(Xn²)  =  5Fn²  =  Ln² − 4(−1)n  =  (φn − ψn)² One number in three coats: Fibonacci supplies the length, Lucas the trace, and their difference is the same ±4 that governs Cassini. Proved from Xn² = Fn²H² = 5Fn²I; symbolic, so it carries all n. This is the dictionary the whole corpus spends: height data on one side, an integer lattice ledger along a √5-ray on the other. forced

§5  Layer three — the semiring, in one breath

The third layer describes the objects the algebra handles: finite multisets of algebraic integers. Each carries two numbers — a magnitude (the Mahler measure M(A) = ∏i max(1, |λi|) ≥ 1) and a charge (χ ∈ ℤ/4ℤ, which compass quadrant each root points to). The golden object Aφ = {φ, ψ} has M = φ and charge {0, 2} — it points only along the real axis. Lesson 2 makes this layer an algebra in its own right; here it is enough to know the three questions it forces, all answered forced:

  • Can the golden mode couple to a copy of itself into a Salem number? No — three independent obstructions forbid it: [R, R] = 0 (no new direction), the charge is trapped in {0, 2} and never reaches {1, 3}, and the "on-circle and expanding" Salem slot is empty because every combination of the reals φ, ψ stays real.
  • What completes the charge to all of ℤ/4ℤ? A unique orthogonal partner, the even quartic K = x⁴ + 5x² − 5: its imaginary roots ±iβ supply the missing {1, 3}, with M(K) = β² = φ²√5 ≈ 5.854.
  • Where does the magnitude bottom out? At the cyclotomic floor M = 1 (Kronecker: roots of unity only), where magnitude collapses and only charge survives. In this algebra M ∈ {1} ∪ [φ, ∞) — isolated by the golden gap.

That last gap is forced inside the algebra's own emission class but open in general mathematics: Lehmer's number ≈ 1.17628 sits inside (1, φ) for a polynomial that 𝔄 cannot emit. Whether the whole band is empty over all polynomials is Lehmer's problem, unsolved since 1933.

Q3Why can the golden mode not close on itself into a Salem number, and what does it need instead?

Arithmetic: the charge is confined to the subgroup {0, 2} and cannot reach the coset {1, 3}. Geometry: the mode lives on the real axis and never climbs onto the complex unit circle. To escape it needs a partner carrying the missing charges — the even quartic K = x⁴ + 5x² − 5, whose imaginary roots supply the quarter-turn.

§2  Squaring is the diagonal of ⊗

Squaring is not a fourth primitive. Look at A ⊗ A: its spectrum is all ordered pairs {λiλj}, and it splits as symmetric plus antisymmetric — the plethysm. Squaring picks out the diagonal.

OA-PLE A ⊗ A = Sym² A ⊕ Λ² A,   ψ²A = diagonal of Sym² A Concretely for the golden seed, cp(R ⊗ R) = (x² − 3x + 1)·(x + 1)² — the first factor is cp(ψ²R) = cp({φ², ψ²}), your Lesson-1 checkpoint polynomial arriving as a theorem output; the second is the wedge Λ². The ψn are semiring endomorphisms — ψn(A ⊕ B) = ψnA ⊕ ψnB, ψn(A ⊗ B) = ψnA ⊗ ψnB, ψm∘ψn = ψmn — and satisfy Newton's identity Tr ψ²A = p₂ = e₁² − 2e₂. Those are the Adams axioms. Calling the package "a λ-ring" is the standard imported name — declared at the naming level, forced at every law.

§3  Character I — the measure, and where it goes tropical

The Mahler measure is a clean homomorphism on ⊕ (M(A ⊕ B) = M(A)M(B)) and intertwines squaring with squaring (M(ψ²A) = M(A)²). On the tensor it is not multiplicative — it is tropical:

OA-M log M(A ⊗ B)  =  ∑i,j (log|αi| + log|βj|)+  (a (max,+) convolution) The naive guess M(A)deg BM(B)deg A is the single most instructive failure in the corpus. Counterexample: golden ⊗ (x² − 2) has spectrum {±√2·φ, ±√2·ψ}, so M = 2φ² ≈ 5.236 — not the factored 2φ ≈ 3.236, because one cross-pair ±√2·ψ falls inside the unit circle and leaves the measure. Whether a cross-term contributes is a fresh boundary decision per pair. forced

The image of the additive character is a monoid, ⟨φ, 2, 3, 5, β²⟩ with β² = φ²√5; its least generator is φ, so nothing lands in (1, φ) — the cost floor, as an intrinsic feature of Character I. forced / computed

§4  Character II — the ℤ/4ℤ charge

Each on-circle root carries a charge (+ℝ↦0, +iℝ↦1, −ℝ↦2, −iℝ↦3), and the laws mirror the operators additively: ⊕ unites, ⊗ takes the sumset mod 4, and ψ² doubles. The showcase object is the content polynomial x⁴ − 1: spectrum {1, i, −1, −i}, charges {0, 1, 2, 3} — the whole group ℤ/4ℤ at M = 1. Doubling sends {0,1,2,3} ↦ {0,2,0,2}, so the odd sector {1, 3} is unreachable in the image of ψ² — the mechanism behind the parity floor of Lesson 3. forced

Q1In one sentence, why is squaring not a fourth primitive operator — and what exactly is ψ²A inside A ⊗ A?
answer

Because A ⊗ A = Sym²A ⊕ Λ²A, and ψ²A = {λi²} is precisely the diagonal i = j inside Sym²A; the off-diagonal part is the exterior square. Squaring is the diagonal restriction of the tensor square — a derived motion, not a new axiom.

Q2Compute M(golden ⊗ (x² − 2)) and say why it beats the naive factored value.
answer

The four products are {√2·φ, −√2·φ, √2·ψ, −√2·ψ}. Only ±√2·φ lie outside the unit circle (√2·φ ≈ 2.29), while ±√2·ψ ≈ ±0.87 fall inside. So M = (√2·φ)² = 2φ² ≈ 5.236, whereas the factored form predicts 2φ ≈ 3.236. The tropical rule floors the inside-pair to 1; the factored rule cannot see the straddle.

Q3Which Mahler measures are fixed by ψ², and what does that single fact say about the whole magnitude axis?
answer

M(ψ²A) = M(A) forces M(A)² = M(A), so M(A) ∈ {0, 1}; since M ≥ 1, only M = 1 is fixed — the cyclotomic (Kronecker) locus, roots of unity. Everything else strictly grows under squaring. The only fixed magnitude is the trivial one, which is why the floor at M = 1 is a genuine boundary and not just a convenient normalization.

Lesson 03 · the charge–measure coupling

Charge Meets Measure: φ for Even, 2 for Odd

The two characters are orthogonal as readouts — yet coupled at the extremal floor. One parity bit governs the coupling: does the charge lattice contain the ray π?

A–Mconsolidation ledger · integer-matrix engine, no float decides

§1  Which groups can a charge be?

An object is charge-admissible if every conjugate has αn ∈ ℝ>0 for some n — all conjugate arguments on a finite rational-angle lattice. The least such n is the charge group ℤ/n. Two structural facts settle the possibilities:

  • Cyclicity. Every charge group is a finite subgroup of ℚ/ℤ, hence cyclic: no ℤ/p × ℤ/p ever occurs. forced
  • Realizability. For every n, the polynomial xn − 2 has charge group ℤ/n, hits all n charges, and has M = 2. So the realizable groups are exactly the finite cyclic ones. forced

The primary decomposition ℤ/n ≅ ∏ ℤ/piei is realized inside the algebra: the Adams operation ψn/piei is the CRT projector onto the pi-primary component. Under ⊗, charge groups combine to ℤ/lcm, and the composition keeps both charges recoverable iff the orders are coprime. forced

§2  The parity-graded floor

Define μ(n) as the least Mahler measure above 1 over objects of charge group ℤ/n. The coupling result:

Thm 5.1 · parity floor μ(n) = φ  (n even),   μ(n) = 2  (n odd) Even is attained constructively by qk(x) = x2k + xk − 1, which has charge group ℤ/2k and M = φ exactly (substitute y = xk: y² + y − 1 gives 1/φ > 0 and −φ < 0). Status is layered honestly: the even attainment and the odd forced lower bound μS are forced; the even lower bound ≥ φ inherits the emission gap, only plausible in general; the odd realized floor μ(odd) = 2 is forced for ℤ/3 and computed beyond.

Why the split? The golden conjugate φ' = (1−√5)/2 < 0 sits at argument π, and π ∈ (2π/n)ℤ iff n is even. So the golden construction — the one thing that realizes M = φ — can only be hosted on an even lattice. This is the π-ray obstruction. forced Odd charge cannot reach φ and must pay M = 2 via xn − 2.

§3  The odd gap is the no-Salem closure

What lives in the band (φ, 2) at odd charge? Nothing admissible — and the would-be occupants are exactly the objects the algebra excludes. A Salem number carries no finite charge group: its unit-circle conjugates are non-cyclotomic, hence at irrational angles. forced

ledger F · β₄ β4 = x⁴ − x³ − x² − x + 1,   M = 1.72208… ∈ (φ, 2),  charge group: none The minimal degree-4 Salem number sits squarely in the odd gap, yet is charge-inadmissible on exact re-classification (its angle is irrational). A two-stage exact search returned no genuine odd-charge object with M ∈ (φ, 2): all 17 loose-screen candidates were irrational-angle Salem/Pisot numbers. So the odd-charge gap coincides with the no-Salem closure. forced for ℤ/3; plausible at all degrees.

The pentagon, worked. At ℤ/5 the elementary ℤ/3 argument fails (the cosines 2cos72° = φ−1 and 2cos144° = −φ are irrational and Galois-coupled). The paper still forces the floor up to μS = 1.3247… (Smyth's plastic number) and closes the degree-four sector at M ∈ {1} ∪ [φ⁴, ∞), φ⁴ ≈ 6.854 — but the residual (non-reciprocal charge-ℤ/5 objects in [μS, 2)) is Lehmer's problem on the pentagon lattice and stays open. Honest discipline: a finite search may extend a claim to computed, never promote it to forced.

§4  The one door — where confinement ends

Every result above governs the abelian semiring (⊕, ⊗, ψn). The matrix commutator [A, B] = AB − BA is deliberately not a generator — and it is the exact boundary. By Shoda / Albert–Muckenhoupt / Laffey–Reams, every trace-zero integer matrix is a commutator; the companion of L(x)(x−1) (Lehmer's polynomial times x−1) is trace-zero and carries Lehmer's number.

ledger L · commutator commutator spectrum: M = 1.17628… ∈ (1, φ),  charge group = ⊥ Below the emission floor and off every angle lattice at once. So no floor, gap, or charge constraint extends across the commutator, and no single theorem governs every free commutator — the goal is unsatisfiable. The no-Salem closure is operation-relative: Salem numbers are excluded by the choice to stay abelian, not by the ambient matrix algebra, which realizes them freely. Confinement is the box; the commutator is the door. forced (Shoda)

Q1Which abelian groups occur as charge groups? Does ℤ/2 × ℤ/2 occur, and what realizes the ones that do?
answer

Exactly the finite cyclic groups ℤ/n — because every finite subgroup of ℚ/ℤ is cyclic. So ℤ/2 × ℤ/2 (non-cyclic) is unrealizable, while every ℤ/n is realized by xn − 2 at M = 2. Note x⁴ − 2 gives the single generator ℤ/4, not ℤ/2 × ℤ/2.

Q2Why exactly φ for even n but a jump to 2 for odd? Name the obstruction.
answer

The π-ray obstruction. The golden conjugate φ' < 0 lies at argument π, and π belongs to the lattice (2π/n)ℤ only when n is even. The measure-φ construction must place a conjugate at π, so it can be hosted only on even lattices. Odd charge cannot reach φ; the cheapest it can do is M = 2 with xn − 2.

Q3The band (φ, 2) is empty of odd-charge measures. What would fill it in general mathematics, and how does that spectrum re-enter here?
answer

Salem numbers fill it — e.g. β4 = x⁴−x³−x²−x+1, M = 1.722 — but they are charge-inadmissible (irrational conjugate angles), so they are absent from the admissible sector. They re-enter through the commutator: every trace-zero integer matrix is a commutator (Shoda), and one carries Lehmer's number at M = 1.17628 ∈ (1, φ) with charge group ⊥. The exclusion is a property of the abelian box, not the matrix algebra.

Lesson 04 · relational charge & gauge rigidity

Relational Charge & Gauge Rigidity

Which part of an object's phase belongs to the number, and which only to a chosen reference ray? Refactor charge into a pure relation, and determine exactly how much survives.

carries forward

§1  The pair charge, and the gauge

Write each conjugate as an oscillator α = |α|·e2πi·t(α) with phase t(α) = Arg(α)/2π. The relational datum is the phase difference, declared whenever it is rational:

gauge · the cocycle trel(α, β) = t(α) − t(β) ∈ ℚ/ℤ This is a ℚ/ℤ-valued cocycle on the coherence groupoid; the absolute charge is a trivialization (a coboundary), and the reference ray Arg = 0 is the gauge. A global rotation ρc: α ↦ e2πicα shifts every absolute phase by c and fixes every relative one. The vocabulary is structural, not physical — no connection to physical gauge theory is implied. declared naming; forced content.

§2  Rigidity — integrality fixes the gauge

Does the relational theory admit objects the absolute theory rejected — systems internally in tune but out of tune with the reference ray? No. For a conjugation-closed spectrum, all pairwise differences rational forces every phase rational.

rigidity theorem relationally admissible  ⇒  charge-admissible,   anchor n ∈ {m, 2m} Over the relational group ℤ/m, the absolute group is pinned to m or 2m — both occur (xm−2 gives n=m; x³+2 gives n=6=2·3). Read this as a canonicity theorem, not a redundancy: the absolute framework of Lesson 3 is the unique natural section of the relational one. The mechanism is integrality — real coefficients make the spectrum conjugation-closed, and the algebraic locus meets each rotation orbit only at rational offsets. forced

So the relational theory adds no new objects on the algebra's own domain — its genuine reach is the inadmissible sector, where there is no section to fix. Every composition law of Lesson 3 descends verbatim: the Adams operations multiply relative charges by k, and the lcm law is secretly relational, with strict growth occurring only through offset cancellation (the tensor product internalizing cross-object phase relations). forced

§3  The parity floor, reference-free

The parity bit of Lesson 3 was never about the ray. It is an intrinsic relation inside the golden pair:

golden relation φ/φ' = −φ²  ⇒   trel(φ, φ') = ½ A sign-twist involution T(p)(x) = (−1)deg pp(−x) shifts every angle by ½, fixes every difference, and preserves the measure, so it exchanges the two anchor sectors at odd m. Hence the relational floor equals the absolute one: μrel(m) = φ for even m (attained), μrel(m) = μ(m) for odd. The universal floor φ is attainable iff ½ ∈ Δ(O), i.e. iff |Δ(O)| is even — the parity bit is the order of a relational class, with no reference ray anywhere. Identity and attainment forced; the floor values stay conditional on the emission gap.

§4  The inadmissible sector — modulus pinning

On the sector the absolute theory discards, the relational theory is genuinely finer: every object carries a computable coherence type, decided exactly on equal-modulus shells by a cyclotomic-contact scan with a provable completeness bound (M ≤ 2(deg P)²). And a general theorem closes the Salem question outright:

modulus pinning irreducible p, one root modulus attained uniquely  ⇒  no two distinct roots differ by a root of unity Proof by Galois transport: an automorphism carries the pinned root to the unique one of its modulus, and a torsion ratio would force a second root of that modulus — contradiction. A Salem number's dominant root τ pins its modulus, so every Salem number is relationally inert: its only coherence is the forced real pair {τ, 1/τ}. And no two distinct Salem numbers circle-lock. Both were open problems of earlier drafts, now closed in the negative. forced

The theorem was written after forty-six certified instances corroborated it: the four canonical Salem tests of degrees 4–10 (including β4 and Lehmer's polynomial, contacts {Φ14} and {Φ110} only), an exhaustive degree-12 small-coefficient census (37/37 Salems inert), and five larger spot checks — cross-checked in a second computer-algebra stack (PARI/GP vs sympy), with no floating-point number anywhere. computed family; forced per instance. Two open successors remain: whether parity is the whole coupling, and the odd floor itself — which the relational refactor proves adds zero new burden to the companion's problem. open

Q1In what precise sense is the absolute charge a gauge-fixing of the relational one — and what is the gauge group?
answer

The relative charge trel is a ℚ/ℤ-valued cocycle on the coherence groupoid; an absolute charge is a trivialization (choice of the constant, i.e. the reference ray Arg = 0). Q2Does making charge relational produce any objects the absolute theory rejected? State the theorem and its one-line mechanism.
answer

No — rigidity. For a conjugation-closed spectrum, all pairwise differences rational forces every angle rational (pair α with its conjugate ᾱ: 2t(α) is rational, so t(α) is). Thus relational admissibility = absolute admissibility, with anchor n ∈ {m, 2m}. It is a canonicity theorem: integrality pins the gauge, and the absolute framework is the unique natural section. The relational language earns its keep only on the inadmissible sector.

Q3Is every Salem number relationally inert? Give the theorem that decides it and how it was corroborated.
answer

Yes — modulus pinning. An irreducible polynomial with a uniquely attained root modulus admits no torsion ratio between distinct roots (Galois transport). A Salem number's dominant root τ is such a modulus, so its only coherence is the real pair {τ, 1/τ} — inert off the real axis. §3  Verify it yourself

No badge in this booklet resolves to anything but an artifact. The emission-algebra primer ships its own harnesses (d1–d7: the power law over n ∈ [−16, 16], the 𝔰𝔩₂ structure constants, the trace duality); the operator suite carries 38 exact checks; the charge–measure coupling regenerates its consolidation ledger (entries A–M) on an integer-matrix engine; and the relational-charge probe runs entirely in integer and rational polynomial arithmetic, cross-checked in PARI/GP. Read the sources and re-run the checks at the archive's test suite and the per-paper papers tree.

A standing caveat. Statements of the form "smallest known" — Lehmer's polynomial above all — are status claims about the literature at a moment in time, not theorems; they are tagged established and inherited, never re-verified by the scripts. And the floor values that read as φ rest on the emission gap (EG), forced only for the integer-quadratic spectrum; the structural results — cyclicity, rigidity, gauge, descent, pinning, the commutator boundary — are unconditional.
