The Two Walls

Mahler, Salem & Lehmer. Two walls — a golden height floor and a four-direction angle lattice — box every Salem number out of what one construction can emit, sidestepping Lehmer's ninety-year-old problem without pretending to resolve it.

Lehmer's problem asks whether the Mahler measure of an integer polynomial can crowd arbitrarily close to 1 from above. It is open since 1933. This course does not attack it. It teaches the corpus's move instead: stop bounding all integer polynomials, build a finitely generated emission algebra, and prove exactly what that machine cannot make. The seven source papers below are its anatomy — the walls, the empty slot the walls create, what that emptiness generates, who is turned away at the door, and how far the same census reaches.

Every load-bearing claim carries a tag naming how strongly it is held — forced proved in the exact structure, computed exhaustive over a stated finite range, declared a principled or interpretive choice, plausible evidence short of proof, open unresolved, established imported from the literature. The tags are not decoration — they are the mathematics. No approximation crosses a decision boundary anywhere in the corpus: every equality, divisibility, and inequality is settled over ℚ, ℚ(√5), or ℚ(51/4).

Lesson 01 · Mahler, Salem & Lehmer

The Measure, and Lehmer's Problem

One definition, one floor that is completely understood, one open problem — and then the pivot the whole corpus descends from: don't solve it, route around it.

exactSturm-decided over ℚ · no float crosses a decision

Reads from Lehmer's Box and The Emission–Gap Theorem.

§1  One definition, three values

For a nonzero p(x) = ad∏(x − αi) ∈ ℤ[x] the Mahler measure is the leading coefficient times the product of the root moduli that escape the unit circle:

def · Jensen M(p) = |ad| ∏i max(1, |αi|) = exp( ∫₀¹ log |p(e2πiθ)| dθ ) The two forms agree by Jensen's formula. Only roots strictly outside the circle contribute; everything on or inside costs a factor of exactly 1.

Hold onto the middle row: only one root escapes, so M is that single root, and the escaping root is the golden ratio φ. That polynomial — the golden seed — is the keystone the entire construction is built on.

§2  The floor at 1, completely understood

Every factor is ≥ 1, so M ≥ 1 always. Kronecker (1857) established settles equality exactly: M(p) = 1 iff every root is 0 or a root of unity — i.e. p is a power of x times cyclotomic factors. So M = 1 is the trivial, fully classified locus; every open question lives strictly above 1.

§3  Smyth's split, and Lehmer's question

A polynomial is reciprocal if its roots are closed under α ↦ α⁻¹ (i.e. xdeg p p(1/x) = p(x)). The whole difficulty concentrates on one side of this split. Smyth (1971) established: every non-reciprocal monic integer p with M > 1 satisfies

Smyth 1971 M(p) ≥ μS = 1.3247179572…  (the plastic number, root of x³ − x − 1) μS is the smallest Pisot number. Smyth closes the non-reciprocal side outright — the trivial and the non-reciprocal cases are both settled. Everything hard is reciprocal.

Lehmer (1933) open asks the reciprocal question: is there a constant c > 1 with M(p) ≥ c whenever M(p) > 1? Or can measures crowd down onto 1? His record-holder is unbeaten after ninety years. Rather than quote its properties we derive them by the house method — the exact fold the papers use everywhere:

C2 · reciprocal fold L(x) = x¹⁰ + x⁹ − x⁷ − x⁶ − x⁵ − x⁴ − x³ + x + 1,  M(L) = 1.1762808182… Reciprocity is an identity: x¹⁰L(1/x) = L(x). Because L is reciprocal of even degree, the substitution t = z + 1/z folds it to the degree-5 trace-down Q(t) = t⁵ + t⁴ − 5t³ − 5t² + 4t + 3. On |z|=1 we have t = 2cos ψ ∈ [−2, 2]; a Sturm count over ℚ gives Q five real roots — 4 inside (−2, 2), 1 in (2, ∞). Unfolding: 8 conjugates on the circle, one real pair (β, 1/β). Exactly one root outside, so M(L) = β.

With an independent irreducibility check, L is a Salem polynomial: irreducible, reciprocal, one root β > 1, one root 1/β, all others on the circle. A Salem number is such a β — a real algebraic integer > 1 whose conjugates lie in the closed unit disk with at least one on it, minimal polynomial reciprocal of even degree 2m ≥ 4. Contrast a Pisot number: one root outside, all others strictly inside. Lehmer's number is the smallest known Salem number, and it sits at 1.176 — strictly below Smyth's μS. The canonical example and the canonical obstruction are the same object.

Dobrowolski (1979) established gives M(p) > 1 + c(log log n / log n)³, but this decays to 1 with the degree n, so it yields no uniform floor. Ninety years, no c.

§4  The pivot: build an emitter, prove two walls

Here is the move. A growth rule charges a description cost of λ log M(θ) to adjoin an algebraic direction θ; the cost has a positive floor iff M is bounded away from 1 over everything the construction can emit. Instead of bounding all of ℤ[x], build a finitely generated system — seeds plus operators — and prove the closure statement

the strategy no Salem number lies in the emission image. With Smyth, this alone forces the cost floor: everything in the band (1, μS) is a reciprocal Salem-type number, so excluding Salems empties the band. This is a closure property of one specific algebra — not a universal height bound — so it never touches Lehmer's problem. The papers call it "sidestepping Lehmer without resolving it," and that phrasing is load-bearing.

The next two lessons build the two walls that enforce it: a height wall at φ (Lesson 2) and an angle wall at the four directions (π/2)ℤ (Lesson 3). Lehmer's number is excluded as an emission, never as a number; the open problem is left exactly where it stood.

Q1Compute M(x² − 3x + 1) by hand, exactly. The roots are (3 ± √5)/2 — both "is it outside?" decisions matter.
answer

The roots are φ² and φ⁻², since φ² = (3+√5)/2 ≈ 2.618 and φ⁻² ≈ 0.382. Only φ² escapes the circle, so M = φ² = φ + 1. (This measure reappears throughout the course as a theorem output.)

Q2Why does the trace-substitution t = z + 1/z require reciprocity before it is even well-defined?
answer

Because t = z + 1/z identifies z with 1/z. The fold is injective only on root pairs {z, 1/z} — and those pairs exist only if the root set is closed under inversion, which is precisely reciprocity.

Q3Why does excluding Salem numbers from the image give a cost floor, when Smyth already handles non-reciprocals?
answer

Smyth floors the non-reciprocal case at μS. The only measures that can live in the band (1, μS) are reciprocal Salem-type numbers. Remove those from the image and the band is empty — so the infimum of emittable M > 1 is ≥ μS > 1.

The Height Wall — the Golden Floor

The first wall is a fence on the height axis: the open strip (1, φ) is empty, and the operators cannot tunnel under it. Where φ comes from, and how far the fence is really forced.

Reads from Lehmer's Box, §"The floor wall."

§1  The base case: an empty strip in degree two

The catalog seeds are size-2 and size-4 companion matrices, so the wall must first hold for quadratics. It does, exactly.

P1-GATE-02/03 No monic p = x² + bx + c ∈ ℤ[x] has M(p) ∈ (1, φ). M = 1 on the cyclotomic quadratics; otherwise M ≥ φ, with equality only at discriminant 5 — the seeds x² − x − 1 and x² + x − 1. A scan of all 625 quadratics with |b|,|c| ≤ 12 confirms both the empty interval and the minimizer.

The proof is a three-case split on c. If c = −1 (hyperbolic reciprocal) the dominant root is (|b|+√(b²+4))/2, smallest at |b|=1 giving exactly φ. If c = 1 the roots are a reciprocal pair: cyclotomic for |b| ≤ 1, reducible at |b| = 2, and ≥ φ² = (3+√5)/2 for |b| ≥ 3. If |c| ≥ 2 the measure is ≥ |c| ≥ 2. In every non-cyclotomic case M ≥ φ. The golden ratio is the smallest Perron number above 1, and here it is the right edge of the minimal gap.

§2  Degree-raising cannot tunnel under the fence

The spectral operators — ⊕ (direct sum), ⊗ (tensor / Kronecker), (·)² (squaring), plus minpoly and re-companion Φ — determine the output spectrum from the inputs'. On the Mahler measure they act by three closed laws:

M multiplicative M(p ⊕ q) = M(p)·M(q),   M(θ ↦ θ²) = M(·)²,   ⊗ composes The set {1} ∪ [φ, ∞) is closed under multiplication and squaring — the pivotal step being φ·φ = φ² = φ + 1 > φ, the golden relation itself. So by induction on word length from the degree-2 base, every emitted measure stays in {1} ∪ [φ, ∞).

The ⊗ case needs the angle wall (Lesson 3) and the field-signature census (Lesson 7) to forbid a small Salem factor; with those, M(𝒮) ⊆ {1} ∪ [φ, ∞) forced for the spectral emission algebra 𝒮, realized at φ. The emission's Mahler spectrum is a discrete sub-semigroup of [φ, ∞) generated by {φ, 2, 3, 5, φ⁴, β²} — limits are not operations, so the construction can never reach the Salem accumulation points of its own Pisot generators.

§3  How far is the fence really forced?

Honesty about scope is the corpus's discipline, so state the strongest unconditional form. The band that carries no plausible tag anywhere in its derivation is (1, μS), not (1, φ):

narrowest true form 1 < M(L) < 5/4 < μS < 4/3 < φ Smyth is unconditional off the reciprocal locus; the angle wall removes Salems kinematically; so (1, μS) is empty with only forced ingredients. φ is forced on quadratics and realized as the minimum, and is the honest headline — but the width from μS to φ buys reach, not the conclusion. Lehmer's number 1.176 sits strictly inside (1, μS), so its exclusion touches no plausible tag: L(5/4) > 0 while (5/4)³ − 5/4 − 1 < 0, separated by exact rationals.

Q1Why is {1} ∪ [φ, ∞) closed under the direct-sum operator ⊕?
answer

⊕ unions root multisets, and Mahler measure is multiplicative over that union: M(p ⊕ q) = M(p)M(q). A product of two numbers each in {1} ∪ [φ, ∞) is again ≥ φ (or 1), never landing in the open gap (1, φ).

Q2The paper calls φ "derived, not posited." In what sense is φ an output here?
answer

Nothing puts φ in by hand. Degree two is fixed by the decision structure (Lesson 4); the smallest non-cyclotomic integer-quadratic measure is then forced to be φ, at discriminant 5. φ falls out the far end as the minimal realizable growth — the theorem, not a hypothesis.

Q3Why does the corpus bother stating the band as (1, μS) when the headline is φ?
answer

Because (1, μS) is empty using only forced ingredients (Smyth + the angle wall), whereas the full φ floor on every sector still leans on a plausible tag off the real sector. Lehmer's number lies inside (1, μS) anyway, so the exclusion — the actual point — rides entirely on the forced part.

The Angle Wall — No Salem Is Emitted

The height wall excludes only small Salems. The angle wall excludes all of them at once, at every degree, by a conserved four-fold charge.

400 · 1126 · 0random circulants · irreducible factors · Salem factors

Reads from The Emission–Gap Theorem and Lehmer's Box.

§1  Angle confinement to (π/2)ℤ

Write arg z for the argument of an eigenvalue. Every catalog seed sits at one of the four cardinal directions.

P2-ANG-01 every catalog eigenvalue has arg ∈ (π/2)ℤ = {0, π/2, π, 3π/2} The six quadratic seeds have real spectra (arg 0 or π). The quartic K = x⁴ + 5x² − 5 solves x² = (−5 ± 3√5)/2: a real pair ±K at {0, π} and an imaginary pair ±iβ at {π/2, 3π/2}. All four directions, all in (π/2)ℤ ≅ ℤ/4ℤ.
P2-ANG-02 ⊗ adds arguments, (·)² doubles, ⊕ unions — all internal to (π/2)ℤ The subgroup (π/2)ℤ is closed under addition (⊗: arg λμ = arg λ + arg μ) and under doubling (squaring: arg λ² = 2 arg λ), but not under halving — no operator takes a square root of an eigenvalue. So the four directions are conserved by every spectral operation.

§2  The two-line exclusion

Combine confinement with one classical fact and the exclusion is immediate.

P2-ANG-03/04/05 on-circle emitted eigenvalue ⇒ 4th root of unity;   Salem on-circle conjugate ⇒ not a root of unity An on-circle point with argument in (π/2)ℤ is one of {1, i, −1, −i}. But a Salem number's on-circle conjugate is never a root of unity — else its irreducible minimal polynomial would be cyclotomic and β itself a root of unity, contradicting β > 1. The two sets are disjoint.

Now the closure argument closes. If a Salem β were an eigenvalue of some integer matrix M ∈ 𝒮, then — since M is an integer matrix — all conjugates of β travel with it, including its on-circle conjugates at irrational angle. But every eigenvalue of M lies at a rational angle in (π/2)ℤ. Contradiction. Watch it on Lehmer's polynomial directly:

C5 · gcd gcd( L(x), x⁴ − 1 ) = 1 Not one of L's ten roots is a fourth root of unity; its eight on-circle conjugates sit at ≈ 62.8°, 107.0°, 137.3°, … — none a multiple of 90°. If β ever appeared as an emitted eigenvalue, all ten conjugates would come along, eight of them violating the wall.
Emission–Gap, Thm main No Salem number is emittable in 𝒮. Equivalently M(𝒮) ⊆ {1} ∪ [μS, ∞). forced The result has four equivalent faces: Mahler (the measure gap), entropy (h = log M, by Lind–Schmidt–Ward, so no small positive topological entropy), channel-spectral (no on-circle expanding channel), and metric (no field with a complex place on the circle).

Therefore Lehmer's number — and by the identical two-line argument every Salem number — is kinematically unreachable. Not screened at runtime; structurally impossible to emit. This is why the angle wall is stronger than the height wall: it is size-blind.

§3  Sealing the doors: circulant and commutator

The angle argument covers the spectral operators, whose effect on the spectrum is forced. Two entry-level operators need separate care, and both are relocated to the trace-down flip t = θ + θ⁻¹ (a Salem is exactly a reciprocal factor whose trace-down has one root past +2 and the rest in (−2, 2) — a "flip-straddle," Sturm-decidable over ℚ).

The circulant is forced with no residual: its eigenvalues lie in a cyclotomic field ℚ(ζn), which is abelian, hence totally real or CM — and a Salem field's signature (2, m−1) is neither. A scan of 400 random integer circulants (1126 irreducible factors) finds zero Salem factors. The free commutator is the one door: by Shoda's theorem any traceless matrix is a commutator, so it could in principle carry a foreign Salem. Lesson 7 closes it structurally by identifying the framework's actual commutator as the self-action adR.

Q1In one sentence: why doesn't the height wall alone exclude Salem numbers?
answer

Salem numbers with M ≥ φ exist in abundance, so the height wall excludes only small Salems; the angle wall excludes all of them, size-blind.

Q2Where does "conjugates travel together" enter the proof, and why is it essential?
answer

An integer matrix has an integer characteristic polynomial, so if one eigenvalue is a root of an irreducible mβ, all its ℚ-conjugates are eigenvalues too. That forces the Salem's forbidden on-circle conjugates into the spectrum — where the angle wall then forbids them. Without integrality the single root β could hide.

Q3Why does the invariant survive squaring but a halving operator would coarsen it?
answer

(π/2)ℤ absorbs doubling: 2·(π/2)ℤ ⊆ (π/2)ℤ. Halving would generate (π/4)ℤ, (π/8)ℤ, … — still rational angles (so the exclusion would persist), but the clean closed form {0, π/2, π, 3π/2} would break. The construction contains no halving operator.

The Conserved Emptiness That Generates

The Salem slot is empty not by accident but by a conserved charge — and a conserved charge is a generator. On this one instance it produces five canonical objects, φ among them.

§1  Two ways to be empty

A dynamic void is a barrier the system could cross but pays not to; such voids leak, and the leak is the interesting object. A kinematic void has zero weight in the algebra the operations act on, enforced by a conserved quantity; nothing approaches it, and the enforcing law never fires. The paper's thesis:

the substrate kinematic emptiness ≡ a conserved charge ≡ a superselection rule declared And the rightmost object is productive: a superselection charge furnishes a grading, a content polynomial, a gap, a normal form, and a conserved current. The Salem slot is the minimal fully-computable instance.

§2  The five objects

The generator is the angle charge of Lesson 3, read as an element of ℤ/4ℤ. Each of the five objects is a theorem with a verification attached.

Object II is the subtle one. An order-n charge produces xn − 1 as the alphabet of allowed on-circle positions; here n = 4. Yet only ±1 are realized on the circle — the ±i sector (charges 1, 3) is present in the algebra but sits off the circle, because the only seed reaching argument ±π/2 is K, whose imaginary place has modulus β = 2.4195 ≠ 1. So x⁴ − 1 records the charge group; x² − 1 would record only its on-circle shadow. Recording the off-circle half is exactly what keeps the claim from over- or under-stating the grading.

§3  The minimal chain: φ is derived, not posited

The load-bearing consequence. Read in the forced direction, three minima cascade:

Prop · minimal chain degree 2 ⇒ ℤ/4ℤ ⇒ φ forced Ternary growth decisions force degree two (# channels = d² − d + 1 = 3 iff d = 2). Order four is the coarsest charge carrying a fourth direction distinct from the three real channels. φ is then the smallest non-cyclotomic quadratic measure — forced out the far end. The golden ratio was not chosen for being golden; degree-two decisions were chosen, and φ is what they produce.

The pattern beneath — kinematic emptiness ≡ conserved charge ≡ superselection — is declared domain-universal (Pauli exclusion, charge superselection, and type preservation share the skeleton), but the five objects are local to this number theory. The table is read down each column, never causally across rows: nothing here claims the angle charge explains Pauli exclusion. Same skeleton, different content; no output is exported across domains.

Q1Why is the content polynomial x⁴ − 1 and not x² − 1, when only ±1 are realized on the circle?
answer

The charge group is genuinely ℤ/4ℤ: the seed K carries charges 1 and 3 on the imaginary axis, but its place has modulus β ≠ 1, so the ±i sector lives in the algebra, off the circle. x⁴ − 1 records the whole charge group; x² − 1 would record only its on-circle shadow and undercount the grading.

The Occupant of the Salem Slot

There is no fourth channel because the trifurcation lives downstairs, in the trace. A would-be Salem is redirected to a totally real Perron number — and at the golden floor its image is √5.

Reads from The Occupant of the Salem Slot.

§1  No fourth channel: the trifurcation is downstairs

The self-action adR = [R, ·] of the golden seed has spectrum {0, +√5, −√5}: three channels, captured (0, M = 1, on-circle), grow (+√5, M ≥ φ, expanding), decay (−√5). A Salem would be a fourth channel — on-circle like captured, yet expanding like grow. Downstairs, in the trace t = θ + θ⁻¹, that is a flat contradiction:

P2-DELTA-02 · three regions |θ| = 1 ⇒ t = 2cos ψ ∈ [−2, 2];   θ > 1 ⇒ t > 2;   the flip cuts ℝ into three "On-circle and expanding" would need |t| < 2 and t > 2 at once — no real t qualifies. The fourth channel is an upstairs artifact of the two-to-one lift t ↦ x, which bundles one grow-trace root with several captured-trace roots into a single irreducible Salem. Working downstairs, the system never forms the bundle.

§2  The redirection map

The object that occupies the slot is the trace redirection τ₀ = β + β⁻¹, the dominant root of the Salem's totally real trace-down T.

Thm · occupant τ₀ = β + β⁻¹ > 2 > φ,   M(T) ≥ τ₀,   τ₀ − 2 = (β−1)²/β (exact, AM–GM) forced Whatever tiny value M(β) = β had, its redirection lands strictly in grow, above φ, regardless. The flip is a branch point: β − 1 ∼ √(t − 2), so a Salem of height 1 + δ is redirected only δ² past the flip — the smaller the Salem, the more tightly its image hugs t = 2.

Nothing is destroyed, only re-sorted: each on-circle conjugate e±iψ freezes into the captured coordinate 2cos ψ ∈ (−2, 2), losing only orientation (the sign of ψ). Growth goes to τ₀; angle goes to 2cos ψ; the trace-down T records both, one-and-the-rest by the flip. And the redirection carries strictly more growth than the Salem it replaces: log τ₀ > log β.

§3  The golden limit and its rate

Now the loop closes with the self-action. Take Salem numbers climbing to the floor, βn → φ (the Salem factors of Sn = xnP − P*, P = x² − x − 1):

P2-UNIF-01 · redirection at the floor βn → φ ⇒  τ₀ = βn + βn⁻¹ → φ + φ⁻¹ = √5 = +√D ∈ spec(adR) forced The slot at the golden floor is filled by √5, the grow generator of adR. The approach is linear with slope τ'(φ) = 1 − φ⁻² = φ⁻¹, and geometric along the canonical family: √5 − τ₀ ∼ (2/√5)·φ−n, so consecutive gaps shrink by exactly φ (verified n ≤ 29).

And the map is forced, not chosen. Because the seed's conjugate is the negative reciprocal (φψ = −1), the trace map θ ↦ θ + θ⁻¹ is the operation computing the self-action's grow eigenvalue: φ − ψ = φ + φ⁻¹ = √5. So the coordinate in which a Salem is defined and the map producing spec(adR) are the same map — the system's own.

§4  The slot rejects filling

The dual question — can anything enter? — has a sharp answer. The angle charge A (= 1 iff all root arguments lie in (π/2)ℤ) is a superselection charge: ⊕, ⊗, squaring, the trace lift, and the monodromy of any closed loop all preserve A, while the slot has A = 0. Every entry route — superposition, suspended superposition, non-linear travel, the lift — is checked and turned away; the only route to A = 0 is a limit, which the finitely generated algebra approaches but never attains. The slot is not a void that can be filled but a space that rejects filling.

Q1Why is "on-circle and expanding" impossible downstairs but seemingly possible upstairs?
answer

Downstairs, on-circle means t ∈ [−2, 2] and expanding means t > 2 — disjoint. Upstairs, the two-to-one lift t ↦ x bundles one grow-trace root with several captured-trace roots into a single irreducible polynomial; that bundle is a Salem. The contradiction is only hidden by the lift.

Because the seed's constant term forces φψ = −1, i.e. ψ = −φ⁻¹, so φ − ψ = φ + φ⁻¹. The grow eigenvalue of adR and the trace-down of the dominant root are literally the same expression √5. The redirection is the system's native coordinate, not an imported modeling decision.

§1  Why the pentagon is the hard analogue of the triangle

A charge group ℤ/nℤ forces every conjugate angle into (2π/n)ℤ. The ℤ/3ℤ proof closes trivially because 2cos 120° = −1 ∈ ℤ: the complex pair's factor is integral the instant its modulus is, forcing the search down to x³ − 2 with M = 2. Nothing couples. The pentagon is different:

Prop · irrational cosines 2cos 72° = φ − 1,   2cos 144° = −φ   — the two roots of x² + x − 1 forced Both are irrational and Galois-conjugate over ℚ under √5 ↦ −√5. A single ±72° pair has an irrational linear coefficient; integrality is repaired only by importing the ±144° pair as its Galois partner, so the √5-parts cancel. That forced coupling is what ℤ/3ℤ never needs.

The general claim — M ∉ (1, 2) for all charge-ℤ/5ℤ objects — holds over the window (quartics |c| ≤ 10, quintics |c| ≤ 4, sextics |c| ≤ 3) but is not promoted.

§1  The ratio object and the scan as a decision

For a monic p with roots α1, …, αn, the ratio object records all ordered root ratios:

Prop · ratio object Ratp(x) = prim Resy(p(y), p(xy)) ∈ ℤ[x],   deg = n², roots {αj/αi} forced Two roots of equal modulus are coherent iff their ratio is a root of unity, and a primitive M-th root of unity forces ΦM | Ratp (Gauss). Since ΦM | P forces M ≤ 2(deg P)², the complete contact scan is a finite decision procedure entirely in ℤ[x] — no float crosses it.

§2  The real-pair ν-reduction, and inertness below two shells

The lemma that does the work: for a real β and non-real α in a conjugation-closed root set, α ≈ β iff α/ᾱ is a root of unity — and α/ᾱ is itself a root of Ratp. So every real×non-real coherence question is decided by the scan of Ratp alone.

Thm · Pisot inertness ≤ 1 pair every Pisot number with ≤ 1 non-real conjugate pair is relationally inert forced The dominant root θ > 1 pins its modulus (all others lie in the open unit disk), so modulus pinning excludes every torsion ratio between distinct conjugates; λ/λ̄ is not torsion, so no real×non-real pair is coherent. This covers every Pisot of degree ≤ 4 and every quintic of "mixed" pattern. The first genuinely open case is the quintic two-pair pattern (θ, λ1, λ̄1, λ2, λ̄2).
